References and further reading

Use this page to find a next explanation, compare approaches, or study a topic in more mathematical depth. The lesson pages were written in original language; these sources informed topic selection and provide routes for further study.

Books

Accessible university-level foundation

OpenStax, University Physics, Volume 1

A free, openly licensed textbook with a full mechanics unit, from units and vectors through rotation, gravitation, and oscillations.

Read the book and view its chapter map ↗

Classical mechanics · Calculus-based

John R. Taylor, Classical Mechanics

A clear progression through Newtonian mechanics, conservation laws, oscillations, central forces, and Lagrangian methods. Use the edition available to you; chapter numbering can vary.

Problem-solving emphasis

David Morin, Introduction to Classical Mechanics

A detailed text with conceptual discussion and challenging problems. It is useful for developing problem-solving fluency after the core ideas are familiar. Use the edition available to you; section numbering can vary.

About the provided PDFs: Taylor’s and Morin’s books were supplied as references for this project. They inform the subject sequence and further-reading notes. The explanations, examples, questions, and interactive tools on this site are written independently.

How to use a reference

  1. Start with the matching lesson on this site to identify the main idea and notation.
  2. Read the related textbook section or course notes, then try problems before checking solutions.
  3. Compare methods and assumptions. Two derivations can look different while predicting the same motion.
  4. Record the edition and section you used, because page and chapter numbering can differ between editions.
