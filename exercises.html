1. Foundations

Units, vectors, and mathematical checks

Problem 1 · Starter

A hiker walks 3 km east and then 4 km north. Find the magnitude and direction of the displacement.

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: Draw perpendicular vector components.

Solution: The components are (3, 4) km. The magnitude is √(3² + 4²) = 5 km. The angle is tan⁻¹(4/3) ≈ 53° north of east.

Problem 2 · Check your reasoning

A proposed formula for distance is x = vt + at. Is it dimensionally consistent? If not, correct the time dependence of the acceleration term.

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: Compare the units of every term with metres.

Solution: vt has units of metres, but at has units (m/s²)s = m/s, so the terms cannot be added. For constant acceleration, the displacement formula is Δx = v₀t + ½at².

Problem 3 · Apply

A displacement vector is 6 m west and 8 m south. Write its components if east and north are positive, then find its magnitude.

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: West and south correspond to negative components in this convention.

Solution: The components are (−6, −8) m. Its magnitude is √(36 + 64) = 10 m.

2. Describing motion

Position, velocity, acceleration, and graphs

Problem 4 · Starter

A cyclist moves from x = 12 m to x = −3 m in 5 s. Find the displacement and average velocity.

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: Keep the sign of the displacement.

Solution: Δx = −3 − 12 = −15 m. Average velocity is Δx/Δt = −15/5 = −3 m/s.

Problem 5 · Apply

A cart starts at 3 m/s and accelerates at 2 m/s² for 4 s. Find its final velocity and displacement during that time.

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: Use the constant-acceleration equations.

Solution: v = v₀ + at = 3 + 2(4) = 11 m/s. Δx = v₀t + ½at² = 3(4) + ½(2)(16) = 28 m.

Problem 6 · Interpret

On a velocity-time graph, velocity changes linearly from 2 m/s to 10 m/s over 4 s. Find the acceleration and displacement.

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: Acceleration is the slope; displacement is the area under the graph.

Solution: a = (10 − 2)/4 = 2 m/s². The area is the trapezoid’s area: ((2 + 10)/2)(4) = 24 m.

3. Forces and Newton’s laws

Free body diagrams and acceleration

Problem 7 · Starter

A 5 kg box has a 25 N force to the right and a 10 N force to the left. Find its acceleration.

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: Find the net force before using Newton’s second law.

Solution: Taking right as positive, Fnet = 25 − 10 = 15 N. Therefore a = Fnet/m = 15/5 = 3 m/s² to the right.

Problem 8 · Draw it

A lamp hangs motionless from one vertical cable. Name and describe the forces acting on the lamp. What is the cable tension?

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: Isolate the lamp; include only forces acting on it.

Solution: The cable exerts upward tension T; Earth exerts downward weight mg. Since the lamp is at rest, net force is zero, so T = mg. If its mass is 2 kg, T ≈ 19.6 N.

Problem 9 · Explain

A swimmer pushes water backward. Identify the third-law partner force and explain why the pair does not cancel on the swimmer’s free body diagram.

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: Ask which object each force acts on.

Solution: The water pushes the swimmer forward. The swimmer’s force acts on the water; the water’s force acts on the swimmer. They act on different objects, so they cannot cancel in a diagram of the swimmer alone.

4. Work, energy, and momentum

Energy changes, impulse, and collisions

Problem 10 · Starter

A 12 N force pulls a sled 5 m in the same direction. How much work does this force do?

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: Use W = Fd cos θ.

Solution: θ = 0°, so W = 12(5)cos 0° = 60 J.

Problem 11 · Apply

A 2 kg object starts from rest. A net work of 16 J is done on it. Find its final speed.

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: Set net work equal to the change in kinetic energy.

Solution: 16 = ½(2)v², so v² = 16 and v = 4 m/s.

Problem 12 · Collision

A 2 kg cart moves at 4 m/s and hits a stationary 2 kg cart. They stick together. Find their shared velocity just after the collision.

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: Conserve total momentum for the two-cart system.

Solution: Initial momentum is 2(4) + 2(0) = 8 kg·m/s. Combined mass is 4 kg, so final velocity is 8/4 = 2 m/s in the original direction.

5. Rotation and oscillation

Torque, angular acceleration, and periods

Problem 13 · Starter

A 20 N force is applied perpendicular to a wrench 0.30 m from the bolt. Find the torque magnitude about the bolt.

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: The force is perpendicular, so sin θ = 1.

Solution: τ = rF sin θ = 0.30(20)(1) = 6 N·m.

Problem 14 · Apply

A wheel has moment of inertia 3 kg·m² and net torque 6 N·m. Starting from rest, what is its angular velocity after 2 s if torque stays constant?

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: First find α from Στ = Iα.

Solution: α = 6/3 = 2 rad/s². Then ω = ω₀ + αt = 0 + 2(2) = 4 rad/s.

Problem 15 · Oscillation

An ideal spring has k = 18 N/m and supports a 2 kg mass. Find its angular frequency and period.

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: For a mass-spring oscillator, ω = √(k/m) and T = 2π/ω.

Solution: ω = √(18/2) = 3 rad/s. T = 2π/3 ≈ 2.09 s.

6. Central forces and advanced mechanics

Orbits, symmetry, and Lagrangian ideas

Problem 16 · Orbit scaling

A satellite moves in a circular orbit. If the orbital radius becomes four times larger, by what factor does its circular speed change?

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: Use v = √(GM/r).

Solution: Speed is proportional to 1/√r. Increasing r by 4 multiplies speed by 1/√4 = 1/2: it becomes half as large.

Problem 17 · Symmetry

A system’s laws are unchanged if the entire experiment is shifted to a different time. Which conserved quantity is associated with this symmetry?

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: This is time-translation symmetry.

Solution: Energy is conserved when the laws do not depend explicitly on time.

Problem 18 · Derive

For a one-dimensional spring, ℒ = ½m q̇² − ½kq². Use the Euler–Lagrange equation to find the equation of motion.

Show hint and worked solution

Hint: Compute ∂ℒ/∂q̇ and ∂ℒ/∂q, then differentiate the first with respect to time.

Solution: ∂ℒ/∂q̇ = m q̇, whose time derivative is m q̈. Also ∂ℒ/∂q = −kq. The Euler–Lagrange equation gives m q̈ − (−kq) = 0, or m q̈ + kq = 0.
