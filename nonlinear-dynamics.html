Lesson 08 · Nonlinear dynamics

Nonlinear motion and chaos

Some systems respond in direct proportion to a push; others do not. Nonlinear interactions can create multiple kinds of motion, sudden changes in behavior, and deterministic trajectories that become hard to predict far ahead.

Advanced · About 20 minutes · Builds on oscillations and phase space

1. Linear and nonlinear models

A linear model responds proportionally: double the input and the response doubles, and combinations of solutions remain solutions. A small-angle spring or pendulum near equilibrium can often be approximated this way.

Nonlinear models include terms that break proportionality. For a pendulum at any angle, gravity produces the restoring term sin θ rather than θ. When θ is small and measured in radians, sin θ ≈ θ, giving the familiar linear approximation.

θ̈ + (g/ℓ) sin θ = 0
small-angle limit: θ̈ + (g/ℓ) θ ≈ 0Undriven, undamped pendulum of length ℓ

The approximation is useful near equilibrium, but it cannot capture every large-angle behavior. At high enough energy, the pendulum can rotate all the way around rather than swing back and forth.

2. Phase space and stability

A system’s state includes enough information to predict its future in the model. For a pendulum, angle θ and angular velocity ω are a convenient pair of state variables. Plotting ω against θ gives a phase portrait.

A fixed point is a state that remains unchanged. A pendulum hanging down is a stable equilibrium: a small disturbance makes it oscillate nearby. Balanced upright, it is unstable: a small disturbance grows and it falls away.

θ̇ = ω    ·    ω̇ = −sin θFirst-order state equations for the ideal undamped pendulum, in scaled time

Trajectories in a phase portrait reveal whether motion oscillates, rotates, approaches an attractor, or escapes from a neighborhood. This picture can show structure that is hard to see from a position-versus-time graph alone.

3. Driving and damping

Real oscillators lose energy through friction or drag. A periodic external force can put energy back in. When the drive competes with damping and the nonlinear restoring force, the motion may settle into a simple rhythm—or become much more complicated.

θ″ + bθ′ + sin θ = γ cos(Ωτ)Dimensionless driven pendulum · τ = t√(g/ℓ); b is damping, γ drive strength, Ω drive frequency

Even when the equation is deterministic, meaning the same state always follows the same rule, the long-term motion can be sensitive to initial conditions. Two starts that are almost identical can eventually produce noticeably different trajectories.

Chaos is not randomness. The equations are deterministic. The difficulty is that tiny uncertainty in the starting state can grow until long-term prediction loses precision.

4. Bifurcations and routes to chaos

As a parameter changes, the qualitative behavior of a system can change. A bifurcation is such a change in the system’s long-term states: a steady state might appear, lose stability, or give way to a repeating cycle.

In some driven systems, repeated period-doubling can lead from one stable rhythm to cycles with twice, then four times, the drive period, and eventually irregular motion. Other routes to chaos also occur; there is no single path shared by every system.

To study a transition, vary one parameter gradually and record the long-term response after transients have faded. Keep initial conditions and all other settings fixed so the comparison is meaningful.

5. Predictability and the Lyapunov idea

One way to quantify sensitivity is to follow two nearby states. If their separation grows approximately like δ(t) ≈ δ₀eλt, a positive Lyapunov exponent λ indicates local exponential separation over that interval.

A positive value does not mean every observable is unpredictable at every time. It gives a timescale over which uncertainty in initial conditions becomes important. Better measurements extend useful forecasts, but they do not remove the underlying sensitivity.

Interpret with care

A numerical simulation is an approximation. Step size, rounding, and model assumptions affect the result. A convincing study compares step sizes, checks conservation laws where appropriate, and asks whether conclusions persist under small changes.

Quick check

1. Why does a small-angle pendulum behave approximately like a harmonic oscillator?

For small angles in radians, sin θ ≈ θ, so the restoring torque is proportional to angular displacement.

2. Can a deterministic system be chaotic?

Yes. Deterministic means the equations specify the evolution; chaos means nearby initial states can separate rapidly, limiting long-term prediction.

3. When comparing two runs to study a parameter change, what should you hold fixed?

Keep initial conditions and all other parameters fixed so the change you observe can be attributed to the parameter you varied.
